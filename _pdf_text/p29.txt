The prediction performance of all variants is presented in Fig. 4. Among these vari-
ants, w/o DV exhibits the most dramatic performance degradation, which highlights
that integrating multi-source biological similarity data is indispensable for compre-
hensively capturing the characteristics of miRNAs and diseases. This result can be
attributed to the strong complementarity between the two biologically heterogeneous
similarity views: the sequence-based view captures structural and evolutionary prop-
erties of miRNAs, while the functional view encodes their regulatory roles in biological
pathways. Similarly, the gene-based and semantic views of diseases provide orthogo-
nal characterizations from molecular and phenotypic perspectives, respectively. When
only a single view is retained, the model loses access to these complementary signals,
leading to embeddings that are biased toward one aspect of biological similarity and
thus insufficient for distinguishing among diverse association types. The dual-view
design, combined with contrastive learning, enables the model to distill view-invariant
features that are robust to view-specific noise, which is particularly critical for fine-
grained type prediction where subtle differences in biological mechanisms must be
captured. Likewise, w/o HGT leads to substantial performance declines, indicating
that the type-aware message passing mechanism effectively models the complex inter-
