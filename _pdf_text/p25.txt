3.3 Hyperparameter analysis
The sensitivity of DHGCMDA performance to hyperparameters is investigated based
on the HMDD v2.0 dataset under the CVtype evaluation setting. Key hyperparameters,
including the temperature parameter t (Eq. 6), the cross-modality contrastive loss
weight λ2 (Eq. 32), and the class weights wk (Eq. 34), are analyzed in detail.
Grid search was performed over t and λ2 with wk fixed to uniform weighting.
Specifically, the search range of λ2 was set to {0.1, 0.3, 0.5 } and that of t to {0.5,
1.0, 3.0}. As shown in Fig. 2(a), all Top-1 metrics of DHGCMDA reached maximum
values when t and λ2 were set to 0.5 and 0.3, respectively. Furthermore, larger values
of λ2 led to performance degradation. This phenomenon may indicate that excessive
emphasis on cross-modality alignment could overshadow discriminative information
captured within individual modalities. The temperature parameter exhibited remark-
able stability across all tested values, demonstrating that the dual-view hypergraph
construction and attention-guided fusion mechanisms provide sufficient discriminative
capacity, thus reducing sensitivity of DHGCMDA to temperature variations.
Using the optimal hyperparameters of t and λ2, three class weighting strate-
gies were further compared: inverse proportional weighting, uniform weighting, and
square-root inverse weighting. As shown in Fig. 2(b), the inverse proportional strategy
exhibited superior performance over the other two strategies, suggesting that appro-
priate emphasis on minority association types facilitates the ability of DHGCMDA to
capture diverse biological mechanisms underlying miRNA-disease associations.
