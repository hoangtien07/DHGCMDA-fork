Despite the promising results, DHGCMDA has several limitations that warrant
discussion. First, the current framework relies on the availability of multiple similarity
measures for both miRNAs and diseases; for novel miRNAs or rare diseases where such
data are extremely sparse, the dual-view construction may degrade to a near-single-
view setting, potentially limiting performance gains. Second, the computational cost
of DHGCMDA scales with the number of nodes and hyperedges, and applying it to
significantly larger biological networks may require architectural optimizations such
as mini-batch hypergraph sampling or more efficient attention mechanisms. Third,
while DHGCMDA learns expressive node embeddings, the interpretability of these
embeddings remains limited; future efforts could incorporate attention visualization
or gradient-based attribution methods to provide mechanistic insights into predicted
association types. Fourth, the dynamic hypergraph update strategy currently employs
a fixed threshold θ and update interval, which may not be optimal across all training
stages; adaptive thresholding strategies could further improve structural refinement.
Since DHGCMDA is a general framework for multi-relational link prediction in
heterogeneous biological networks, it can be readily extended to other tasks such as
drug-drug interaction type prediction and lncRNA-disease association type prediction.
In future work, efforts will be directed toward addressing the limitations identified
above: integrating additional biomedical information sources such as miRNA-gene
interactions to mitigate data sparsity, adopting positive-unlabeled learning strategies
to better handle uncertainty in negative samples, and exploring adaptive graph updat-
ing mechanisms and model interpretability techniques to enhance both structural
refinement and biological insight.
