sponding interaction networks among these entities. These databases have significantly
enriched the current understanding of miRNA functionality and regulatory mecha-
nisms, while also laying an essential foundation for the development of computational
methods for the prediction of miRNA-disease associations. In parallel, the rapid evo-
lution of representation learning paradigms, from traditional machine learning to deep
graph models and multi-modal fusion, has further accelerated progress in biomedical
data analysis [9].
Computational methods developed for miRNA-disease association prediction have
undergone successive stages of evolution. Similarity-based methods [10, 11] were pred-
icated on the assumption that functionally similar miRNAs tend to correlate with
phenotypically similar diseases, and vice versa, yet such methods are inherently con-
strained by two critical limitations: an excessive reliance on similarity metrics and
a fundamental inability to capture the intricate nonlinear relationships embedded in
biological systems. Traditional machine learning methods, for instance, support vector
machines and matrix factorization, achieved enhanced prediction accuracy; however,
they typically processed miRNAs and diseases in an isolated manner and failed to
explicitly model the intricate network architectures inherent to biological systems.
